# FareSight QA Report 1 (first-time-user pass)

Tester persona: non-technical airline-booking revenue manager seeing the app for the first time. UI only, no code or API reading.
Environment: http://localhost:5055, in-app browser. The app's idea of "today" was 17 Nov 2026 (India earliest date 18 Nov 2026).
Note for developers: the build changed while I was testing (about halfway through, the tagged cards switched from showing the "FARESIGHT" price to "EXPECTED LOW"). Findings marked **[old build]** were reproduced on the first build and I could not reproduce them on the later one. Please check that the build you demo is the later one.

## (a) Verdict

**Not yet. Yes only with fixes (and only if the later build is what is shown).** The look and feel is polished, the layout holds up on phone and tablet, and the pages are unusually honest about limits. But a client will notice four things within five minutes. (1) Most flights show huge predicted rises (Delhi to Mumbai 17 Dec: 255 of 343 flights "higher than listed", up to +290%), which looks like a broken model. (2) The "Price may drop" and "Big saves" filters exclude the flights whose cards say "LARGE DROP down 55%", and the headline price on a card means two different things. (3) GO FIRST and Vistara are shown as bookable in Dec 2026 and the only hint that the model runs on 2022/2023 scraped data is the jargon "2023 price level". (4) The Model & Insights page tells the client that the model never saw a flight's price move over time, which undercuts the "drop" and "big save" tags. On the first build, 7 of 34 filtered flights showed a rise or no change under a "May drop" or "BIG SAVE" tag, which would have been a blocker. This was fixed mid-session. Approve has no explanation and no "nothing is booked" message.

## (c) What worked well

- Clear, polished first screen: the headline and one-line explanation give a quick idea of what it does. The airplane route graphic is attractive. Dark and light themes both look good (switch in the top right), and the dark/light toggle works.
- Flight cards are easy to scan: airline, flight number, departure-arrival line with stops, listed vs FareSight, arrow and amount.
- Filters and counts agree: "Price may drop (34)" and "Big saves (23)" matched the cards on every route I checked (about 40 route/date combos in India and 100 in the US), and the labels update when airline or stops change (IndiGo gave 12 and 7; IndiGo non-stop gave 4 and 1). Pagination "Showing 11 to 20 of 34" was correct on every page.
- Empty state "No flights match those filters" with a working "Clear filters" link.
- The US tab, the US results banner ("snapshot ... model improves only slightly on 'the price stays the same'") and the Model page "How the May drop tag works" are candid. This honesty is a strength, if the rest of the page agrees with it.
- Layout holds at 375x812 and 768x1024: no horizontal scroll on the search page, details or any Model tab; cards restack neatly.
- Keyboard: filters, checkboxes and pager are reachable with Tab; focus ring is visible; Space ticks the checkbox.
- Rapid repeated clicks on Search did not duplicate results or break the page.
- Settings: change was applied to the next search as promised (header chip and counts); reset restored defaults.
- Fonts and CSS load fast; no JS errors besides the server-down moments.

## (d) The 5 changes I would make first

1. **Make the tags, filters and card number tell one story (F01-F04).** Define "May drop", "Big save" and "Large drop" with one sentence each on the page, put the same price (and same label) in the big slot on every card, and make sure no flight with a big drop on its card is outside the filter. Keep the later build's rule that every tagged card shows a lower price, and fix the 1-point rounding differences.
2. **Fix the credibility of the numbers (F05, F06).** A first screen where most flights are "up 39% to up 290%" and one airline has one price will sink the demo. Either explain the listed price on the card ("airline's typical last-minute price") or scale and cap the display. Say plainly on the Search page "based on 2022/2023 scraped fares, not live" and drop or mark defunct airlines (GO FIRST, Vistara) and invalid future dates.
3. **Say what Approve does, or remove it (F09).** Add one line ("Shortlist only. Nothing is booked.") next to the button, a count after "Approve all", a one-click "clear approvals", a warning (or keeping approvals) when a new search is run, and hide "Approve all" on empty pages.
4. **Reconcile the accuracy story (F07, F08, F23).** Put the three accuracy numbers (93 / 87 / 66) and the "never saw price move over time" limit in one visible sentence on the Search page; remove or caveat "by N days out" tags beyond the tested horizon (US: 125 days vs 14 tested) and fix the "7 days: better" contradiction.
5. **Translate for non-technical readers (F12, F13, F15, F22).** Replace raw terms (te_flight, change_asc, "Airline code (internal) 8", "checkpoint"), explain "Days before departure 1", "model's price for today" and "Typical price", rename or merge the two near-identical sort options, and add friendlier error text ("We couldn't reach the service, please try again"). Also make the URL carry the search so Back and Refresh work (F17).
