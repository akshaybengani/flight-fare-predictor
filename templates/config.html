How the "May drop" tag works

The tag is a model estimate, not a promise. A flight gets it only when all of these hold:

  1. Only the future counts. The model prices the flight at several dates between today and departure. Dates already past are ignored.
  2. A dip must last. The expected low is the lowest price the model keeps for two dates in a row, so one odd point cannot trigger the tag.
  3. It must be a real fall. That low must be at least the percentage above below the listed fare and below FareSight's own estimate for today. Today's estimate is the fair comparison, because the listed fare is a scraped fare and can differ from what the model thinks today.
  4. The card agrees. The big FareSight price on the card must also be below the listed fare, so a May drop card never shows a rise. The expected low appears as a second, green line.
  5. Big save. A May drop is a big save when the expected low is below the listed fare by the big-save percentage, or by the rupee amount above provided that is also a fall of at least 10%.

Most fares are expected to rise toward departure, so on many routes and dates no flight is tagged. That is the honest result, not a fault.
